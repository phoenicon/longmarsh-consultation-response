Subject: Longmarsh Green Energy — the Data Centre Covenant, local democratic accountability, energy demand and Norfolk's cumulative solar and infrastructure proposals

Dear Mr Narayan,

I am writing as a resident of Wiggenhall St Mary Magdalen in west Norfolk about the proposed Longmarsh Green Energy development. I recognise the Government's aims for AI, digital infrastructure, energy security and investment. I am not opposed to those aims or to renewable energy. But I am concerned about the scale of this proposal, its rural location and the long-term effects on farming, countryside and local communities.

Longmarsh comprises a Western Site near Tilney St Lawrence, including the data centre, solar and batteries, and a separate Eastern Site near Fincham and Barton Bendish, also with solar and batteries. The sites would be connected by overhead and underground infrastructure and linked to Walpole. It is a geographically extensive scheme, not simply a data centre with panels immediately beside it.

The energy case needs transparent evidence. The reported estimate of around 6,000 GWh annual data-centre demand and the reported suggestion that solar might provide around 20% should be confirmed or corrected. The applicant should publish the hourly and seasonal energy balance, grid imports, battery capability, offshore-wind arrangements and backup generation. Installed solar capacity is not the same as continuous output.

The proposal should also be considered alongside other major Norfolk solar developments, including High Grove, The Droves and East Pye, and the paused Tasway proposal. Published figures suggest thousands of hectares of land are involved, although project boundaries, panel areas and infrastructure areas are not directly comparable.

There should be a regional assessment of the cumulative effects of major energy and data-centre developments across Norfolk, Lincolnshire and Cambridgeshire, including the wider Fenland agricultural area. These counties contain some of the country's most productive farmland and form an interconnected agricultural, drainage and ecological landscape. The assessment should consider the combined effects on productive farmland, food production, soil quality, agricultural viability, water resources, irrigation, drainage systems, roads, wildlife habitats, landscape and local communities.

This is particularly important because the flat landscape and relatively good sunlight that make the area attractive for solar development must be considered alongside its low rainfall and existing water constraints. Farmers already operate within a tightly managed network of low-lying rivers, drains, ditches and dykes, with water levels and abstraction carefully controlled. The availability of water for crop irrigation is therefore a significant practical constraint. The cumulative demands of solar and battery developments, data centres and associated infrastructure could place further pressure on water resources and drainage systems, with consequences for agricultural production and flood management.

The actual area of Grade 1, Grade 2 and Grade 3a agricultural land affected must be established through detailed, site-specific surveys, rather than relying solely on broad predictive maps. The assessment must also consider the cumulative loss and fragmentation of farmland across the wider region, rather than examining each development in isolation. The Government should explain who is responsible for commissioning and coordinating this regional assessment, what geographical area it will cover, and how its findings will influence decisions on individual projects.

I ask Government to ensure that the applicant assesses practical alternatives before the design is settled. These include local rooftop and car-park solar with batteries, coordinated through a Virtual Power Plant and providing clear benefits to participating households and community buildings; recovery of data-centre waste heat for a local district-heating network; and the feasibility of Rolls-Royce Small Modular Reactors or other firm low-carbon power. None should be assumed suitable without proper assessment, and none should be dismissed without evidence.

The SMR option also raises an industrial question. Rolls-Royce SMR has an ambition for substantial UK supply-chain content, while conventional solar-module manufacture is heavily concentrated overseas. The applicant should compare actual UK manufacturing, jobs, skills, procurement and export benefits, separating confirmed UK contracts from targets and overseas sourcing.

Water and heat deserve particular attention. A closed-loop cooling description does not by itself explain the complete heat-rejection system or all water use. The applicant should assess non-potable and seawater options where feasible, and whether recoverable heat can be supplied to nearby homes, schools, churches, care facilities, farms and businesses through a district-heating network.

Residents also need to see what the development would look like. Please ensure that the applicant publishes matched present-day and completed-project images from the same viewpoints, accurate plans and elevations showing existing and proposed ground levels, and scaled comparisons using a standard double-decker bus. The images should include buildings and plant, raised ground, solar arrays, fences, lighting, batteries, substations and the actual design and height of pylons and overhead lines.

The November 2026 Budget announcement of a new Data Centre Covenant makes these questions particularly important. The Government says that strategic data-centre projects seeking enhanced Government support will be expected to meet stringent tests covering community investment, energy costs, support for British industry and jobs, and national security. I welcome the recognition that major data-centre developments must be judged against their wider effects and the benefits they bring to the country and to host communities. But those principles must mean more than a statement of intent. I ask Government to explain how the Covenant will be applied to Longmarsh, whether the project falls within its scope, and how each test will be assessed, monitored and enforced. If Longmarsh is not formally within scope, I ask that the same standards are applied to it.

Most importantly, I ask for an unequivocal commitment from Central Government that the views of local people and their elected representatives will not be treated as a procedural formality. Local planning concerns, objections, evidence and reasonable alternatives must be properly considered and answered. This must include the cumulative effect across Norfolk of Longmarsh alongside High Grove, The Droves, East Pye and any other substantial solar, battery, data-centre and grid-infrastructure proposals. The county-wide consequences cannot be assessed by considering each project in isolation.

I recognise that a project of this scale may ultimately be determined through the national Development Consent Order process, and I am not suggesting that local objections should automatically amount to a veto. But national importance must not become a reason to disregard local evidence or to override local decisions without meaningful accountability. If Ministers are minded to reach a decision that conflicts with the views or recommendation of the local planning authority, or with substantial concerns raised by affected communities, I ask for a commitment that they will first hear directly from local residents and local representatives at an open and properly recorded meeting or hearing. Ministers should then publish a clear, reasoned response explaining how those representations, the local authority's position, county-wide cumulative impacts and the alternatives put forward have been weighed.

Residents should be reassured that taking part in local consultation, submitting detailed objections and engaging with their councils can genuinely influence the process. It must not become a box-ticking exercise in which people are invited to comment, their evidence is collected, and a national decision is then taken regardless without Ministers having heard from them. I therefore ask Government to set out what safeguards will ensure that local participation has practical weight, that significant local concerns receive a substantive response, and that any decision to depart from local views is transparent and accountable.

I am concerned that consultation may be asking residents to comment before essential studies and design details are available. I ask Government to support a further meaningful opportunity to comment once the principal assessments have been published and before the DCO application is finalised.

I also ask for a clear commitment that no physical construction or enabling works begin until all necessary consents and permissions are in place, pre-commencement requirements are met and any applicable legal challenge period or proceedings have concluded, with any such commitment secured through an effective legal mechanism.

I am not asking you to prejudge the planning decision, nor to give local objections an automatic veto. I am asking for a clear assurance that national objectives for AI, energy and economic growth will not simply override the considered views of local communities. The people who live with the consequences must be heard directly before Ministers make a decision that sets those views aside. Local consultation must be capable of influencing the outcome, and any departure from local recommendations or material objections must be fully explained. The Data Centre Covenant should reinforce these safeguards, not sit alongside a planning process in which local voices can ultimately be ignored.

I enclose my Schedule A of questions to the developer. I would be grateful for a response explaining how Government intends to ensure that these issues are addressed through the planning and policy process.

Yours faithfully,

xxxxx  
Wiggenhall St Mary Magdalen, Norfolk

### A proper sequence for alternatives and public consultation

I ask the Government to ensure that the reasonable alternatives raised during the present consultation are properly investigated, evidenced and answered before the developer settles the scheme it intends to submit as its final application. These should include alternatives relating to location, scale, layout, the relationship between the data centre and solar sites, grid and cable routes, water supply and cooling, waste-heat use, and other ways of meeting the stated objectives.

The developer should publish its assessment, explain which alternatives are considered feasible and give evidence-based reasons for rejecting those it does not take forward. Only after this work should the final scheme be settled and published for a further meaningful consultation, with enough time and information for residents and local authorities to comment while there remains a genuine opportunity to influence the proposals. I do not believe consultation is meaningful if the principal choices have already been made.

### A consolidated Government response and clear ministerial responsibility

Please identify the Minister with overall responsibility for coordinating the Government's consideration of Longmarsh, and the lead department. I would be grateful if that Minister could coordinate a single, consolidated substantive response to this representation and its accompanying questions, drawing on all relevant departments and agencies.

The response should address the Government's position across planning and national infrastructure, energy security, data-centre policy and the Data Centre Covenant, agricultural land and food production, water resources, environmental protection, local government and community interests. Please identify which department or public body is responsible for each issue, explain any matter that cannot yet be answered, and give a date by which a substantive response will be provided. Separate acknowledgements or disconnected departmental replies would not provide the clarity or accountability being requested.

### Visual information residents can understand

Please ensure the developer is asked to publish a comprehensive, route-based visual presentation of the development. Residents should be able to see, through a sequence of accurate images, what the scheme would look like as they travel through the affected area, not merely from a small number of selected viewpoints.

The images should show existing and proposed views from principal roads, villages, public rights of way and other publicly accessible locations, including the combined appearance of solar arrays, data-centre buildings and plant, batteries, substations, pylons, overhead lines, fencing, access roads, CCTV, lighting, bunds, raised platforms and screening. They should include construction and completed views, night-time views where relevant, accurate dimensions and ground levels, and longer-distance views wherever structures may be visible. They should be published in time to inform the further consultation on the final proposals.

### No enabling works before alternatives and permissions are resolved

I seek explicit Government reassurance that no enabling, preparatory or construction works associated with Longmarsh will be allowed to commence until reasonable alternatives have been fully explored and reported, the final proposals have been subject to meaningful consultation, all necessary statutory consents and permissions have been formally granted, and applicable pre-commencement requirements have been satisfied.

This should include site clearance, vegetation removal, fencing, access roads, groundworks, changes to drainage or land levels, cable routes, substations and grid infrastructure. Please confirm which authority or Government body would secure and enforce this safeguard, and how it would be legally secured. I recognise that the precise legal controls depend on the relevant consents; I am asking for a clear commitment through the appropriate mechanisms, not assuming that a blanket rule already applies in every circumstance.

I would be grateful for a single coordinated response from the identified lead Minister, with a clear indication of the next steps and timescale.

### Wildlife habitats and biodiversity

I am also deeply concerned about the potential erosion, destruction and fragmentation of significant wildlife habitats. I ask Government to ensure that the assessment considers the integrity of whole habitats and ecological networks, not merely whether individual protected species have been recorded. This should include wetlands, ditches, watercourses, hedgerows, mature trees, grasslands, woodland, farmland habitats and corridors connecting them.

Please confirm how Government will ensure that irreplaceable habitats and locally important habitat networks are identified before the design is settled; that the mitigation hierarchy gives priority to avoiding harm; and that biodiversity net gain is not treated as a substitute for avoiding destruction that cannot genuinely be reversed. Monitoring, management, long-term funding and enforceable remedies should be specified.

### Residential property values and a fair system of redress

I ask Government to address a matter of serious concern to local householders: the potential for the Longmarsh data centre, extensive solar development and associated infrastructure to reduce the value and saleability of homes in the surrounding area. If the project is approved and subsequently causes a marked and demonstrable reduction in local property values, what redress would be available to affected households? Would Government expect the developer to provide compensation, a property purchase or protection scheme, or another form of remedy? If no such provision is envisaged, please say so clearly and explain why residents should bear that risk.

What arrangements will be made now to establish an independent, transparent baseline of property values before the scheme progresses? Will Government require or encourage the developer to fund a professionally designed baseline and follow-up study, using actual comparable sales and independent valuations, and publish results at key stages? Residents may preserve dated Rightmove and Zoopla estimates, but these are indicative portal estimates, not formal valuations. A robust baseline should not depend on screenshots alone.

I also ask Government to clarify the Council Tax position. In England, Council Tax bands are based on estimated market value at 1 April 1991, and the Valuation Office Agency is responsible for banding, not the Borough Council. Government guidance recognises that a material physical change in the locality can in some circumstances justify a band review if it materially reduces property value. Will Government and the VOA provide clear guidance for residents if Longmarsh causes such a change, and explain what evidence and process would apply? Will the Council support residents by directing them to the correct process and supplying relevant local information?
