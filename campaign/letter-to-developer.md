# LONGMARSH GREEN ENERGY

Personal submission to EPL Norfolk Limited | November 2026

To: EPL Norfolk Limited

Subject: Request for clear information, proper assessment of alternatives and protection for local communities

Dear Sir/Madam,

I am a resident of Wiggenhall St Mary Magdalen. I am writing as an individual, not on behalf of an organisation. I am not seeking to organise a campaign or collect signatures. I am, however, deeply concerned about the scale and location of the Longmarsh proposals and what they could mean for the countryside, farming, wildlife, roads, water resources and the people who live here.

I recognise the need for energy infrastructure and digital capacity. I am not opposed to renewable energy or data centres in principle. But describing a project as nationally important does not, by itself, show that this particular development, in this location and at this scale, is necessary or proportionate. The applicant should demonstrate that it has properly considered reasonable alternatives.

Longmarsh has a Western Site near Tilney St Lawrence, where the data centre, solar and batteries are proposed, and a separate Eastern Site near Fincham and Barton Bendish, also with solar and batteries. The sites would be connected by overhead and underground infrastructure and linked to Walpole. This is a geographically extensive scheme, not simply a data centre with panels immediately beside it.

Please explain the energy case in plain terms. The reported estimate of around 6,000 GWh annual data-centre demand and the reported suggestion that solar might meet around 20% of that demand need to be confirmed or corrected. I want to understand how much electricity will actually be generated, how much will be imported from the grid, what role offshore wind and batteries will play, and what happens when solar and wind output are low.

I ask you to assess alternatives before the design is settled. These should include a funded local solar-and-battery programme linked through a Virtual Power Plant, recovery of data-centre waste heat for a local district-heating network, and the feasibility of Rolls-Royce Small Modular Reactors or other firm low-carbon power. I am not suggesting that any one option is automatically suitable. I am asking for proper comparisons of land use, reliability, water, cost, carbon, timescale, safety and local benefits.

A local solar and battery programme could allow homes, farms, schools, churches, village halls and businesses to benefit from funded installations, cheaper electricity or other clear financial arrangements, while potentially reducing the need for ground-mounted panels on farmland. A Virtual Power Plant would need proper network and commercial arrangements; please explain how it could work in practice and what participating residents would actually receive.

Please assess whether waste heat from the data centre could be captured and used to heat nearby homes and public buildings through insulated pipes and heat pumps. This should be considered at the design stage, not left as a possible future retrofit. The data centre would still need reliable cooling when there is no demand for heat, and the assessment should explain how that would be managed.

The SMR question is also relevant. Rolls-Royce is developing a UK-designed 470 MW-class small modular reactor. I appreciate that it is not yet an established off-the-shelf power station and that nuclear safety, cooling, flood risk, planning, waste and delivery times would all need careful assessment. Nevertheless, please explain whether it has been considered as a possible source of firm electricity, alone or alongside renewables, and compare UK manufacturing and employment benefits with the supply chains for imported solar and battery equipment.

The cumulative impact across Norfolk also needs to be faced. High Grove, The Droves and East Pye are major proposals; Tasway is paused; and smaller schemes have also been approved. Published project areas are measured on different bases, so they should not simply be added together and described as panel coverage. But the combined scale is substantial. I want to know how much land is actually covered by panels and electrical infrastructure, how much is Best and Most Versatile farmland, and what the combined effect will be on food production, landscape, roads, drainage, wildlife and communities.

I am particularly concerned about productive agricultural land. Please provide field-by-field surveys, not just predictive maps, and quantify effects on crop output and farm businesses. Any claim that farming will continue beneath or between panels should identify what will actually be produced and how that output will be measured.

The physical scale must also be shown honestly. Your current proposals page describes solar panels up to 4.5 metres high, while the Scoping Report describes data-centre buildings up to 25 metres including plant. Please provide matched images from the same viewpoints showing the present landscape and the finished development. Include scaled plans, elevations and cross-sections, and a standard double-decker bus to help residents understand the height of buildings, raised ground, fences, lighting columns, batteries, substations and pylons. Show the actual overhead-line designs along the routes.

I also want proper answers on flood risk, drainage, water use, noise, night lighting, road disruption, aviation, wildlife, fire safety and emergency-service costs. Promises about mitigation, compensation, community benefits or restoration should be specific, funded and legally secured, and should remain in place if ownership or operators change.

I do not believe residents can make an informed assessment while important studies and design details remain unavailable. Please publish a complete register of all studies, show what is finished and what remains outstanding, and give dates for publication. I ask for a further opportunity for residents to comment on the completed assessments and material design changes before the DCO application is finalised and submitted.

I enclose Schedule A, which sets out my questions in plain English. Please answer each question separately, with evidence references. A general reference to a future report is not an answer. Where information is unavailable, please say so and give a date when it will be provided.

I also ask you to confirm that no physical construction or enabling works will begin until all necessary consents and permissions are in place, pre-commencement requirements have been met and any applicable legal challenge period or proceedings have concluded. If you cannot give that commitment, please explain precisely what work you believe could begin earlier and how irreversible changes would be prevented.

I hope you will treat these questions as a genuine request for information and a fair assessment of alternatives. The countryside, farms and communities affected are not empty space on a project plan. They are where people live, work and have built their lives.

Yours faithfully,

xxxx  
Wiggenhall St Mary Magdalen, Norfolk

Enclosure: Schedule A — Short questions for the applicant

### Additional requirements: alternatives, visual evidence and consultation sequence

The alternatives raised during this initial consultation must be properly investigated and answered before you settle the scheme intended for the formal application. It is not enough simply to record suggestions and dismiss them without evidence.

Please assess reasonable alternatives relating to the location, scale, layout and configuration of the development; the siting and relationship of the data centre, solar generation and batteries; electricity connection routes; water supply and cooling; waste-heat recovery; and other ways of meeting the project's stated objectives. For each reasonable option, explain whether it is feasible and, if rejected, why, with supporting evidence.

Publish the findings and your responses before the final scheme is settled. Then provide a further meaningful consultation on the resulting final proposals, with sufficient time and information for residents and local authorities to understand what has changed and to comment while decisions can still be influenced.

### A route-based visual presentation of the development

I specifically request a comprehensive pictorial presentation showing what residents, visitors and people travelling through the affected area will actually see. This should not be limited to a few selected artist's impressions.

Please provide a sequence of verified, geographically identified viewpoints along principal roads and routes through and around the affected area, as well as views from villages, homes where appropriate, public rights of way and other publicly accessible locations. Show the existing landscape and the proposed development from the same camera position, direction, lens/view angle and season where practicable.

The presentation must show the combined appearance of all visible elements: solar arrays, data-centre buildings and rooftop plant, battery compounds, substations, pylons and overhead lines, underground cable works where visible, access roads, fencing, CCTV, lighting, landscaping, screening, bunds and any raised platforms. Include construction-stage views, completed views, and night-time views where lighting would be perceptible.

Use accurate dimensions, distances, ground levels and sightlines. Clearly identify the height of each structure, including any plant or equipment above the main building roofline, and distinguish existing ground from proposed raised ground. Include wide views and longer-distance viewpoints wherever structures may be visible; do not impose an arbitrary 3km limit where evidence indicates visibility beyond it. Please confirm the proposed pylon heights and designs: the Council's comments refer to 50m pylons, but I ask you to publish the actual route-specific design rather than assume that figure is final.

These visual materials should be published early enough to inform the further consultation on the final proposals, not after the principal design decisions have effectively been made.

### No premature enabling or construction works

I request a clear, legally secured commitment that no enabling, preparatory or construction works associated with the scheme will commence until reasonable alternatives have been fully explored and reported, the final proposals have been subject to meaningful consultation, all necessary statutory consents and permissions have been formally granted, and all applicable pre-commencement requirements have been satisfied.

This should cover site clearance, vegetation removal, fencing, access roads, groundworks, changes to drainage or land levels, cable routes, substations, grid infrastructure and any other activity that could alter the land or affect its existing use. Please identify the authority responsible for securing and enforcing this safeguard. This is a request for an explicit commitment through the appropriate consent mechanisms, not an assumption that an identical blanket restriction already applies in every circumstance.

Please treat these matters as substantive requests and respond to them individually, with supporting evidence and a timetable.

### Property values, compensation and Council Tax

I am also concerned about the potential effect of this development on the value and saleability of homes in the surrounding area. There is significant local concern that the scale and nature of the proposed data centre, solar arrays and associated infrastructure could result in a marked loss of residential amenity and property value.

Please commission and publish an independent assessment of the likely effect on residential property values, covering the Western and Eastern Sites, overhead lines, substations, construction traffic, noise, lighting, landscape change and cumulative effects. The assessment should identify the affected area, establish a transparent baseline using comparable local sales and explain how it will distinguish any Longmarsh effect from wider housing-market movements.

What baseline property-value information have you captured before development? Will you fund or support an independently managed baseline study now, with periodic updates at consent, construction and operation stages? Please explain what evidence residents should preserve, including dated portal estimates, asking and sale prices and independent valuations.

If properties experience a demonstrable, material loss in value attributable to the development, what compensation, mitigation or other redress would be available from the developer? If you consider no compensation would be available, please explain the basis for that position and whether any voluntary property-value protection or purchase scheme has been considered.

Please also explain whether you have considered the potential consequences for Council Tax banding and whether you will support residents seeking guidance or review from the Valuation Office Agency where there is evidence of a material change in the physical state of the locality.

### Wildlife habitats and ecological integrity

I am concerned not only about individual protected species but also about the loss, destruction, fragmentation and long-term degradation of important wildlife habitats. Please identify all habitats within and around both sites and the connecting infrastructure, including ancient or veteran trees, hedgerows, watercourses, wetlands, ditches, grasslands, woodland, farmland habitats and habitat corridors.

Please publish baseline ecological surveys, seasonal survey dates, habitat condition and extent, the location of protected and irreplaceable habitats, and the likely direct, indirect and cumulative effects during construction and operation. Explain how habitat connectivity and movement between sites will be maintained, what habitat will be permanently lost or degraded, and what cannot realistically be recreated.

Please explain how the mitigation hierarchy has been applied: avoidance first, then minimisation, restoration and only then compensation. Biodiversity net gain should not be presented as making the destruction of irreplaceable or locally significant habitat acceptable. Please identify who will monitor habitat outcomes, for how long, and what enforceable action follows if promised outcomes are not achieved.
