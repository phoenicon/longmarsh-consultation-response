# LONGMARSH GREEN ENERGY

## Schedule A — Questions from a concerned resident

20 November 2026

### Purpose and how to answer

I am a local resident seeking clear answers about the proposed development. Please answer each question separately and in plain English. Where technical terms are needed, explain them.

For each answer, identify the supporting evidence and give the exact report title, date and page or paragraph. State what remains unknown, who is responsible and when the information will be available. Where relevant, explain local effects, mitigation, funding, monitoring and enforcement.

If information is not yet available, say so, explain why and give a realistic publication date. If a question does not apply, explain why.

## A. Consultation, information and decision-making

- **A1:** List every survey, study and assessment required for the project, including both sites and all connecting infrastructure.
- **A2:** For each, state whether it is complete, underway, planned or not commissioned; identify the responsible party and expected completion date.
- **A3:** Publish completed reports, maps, drawings and underlying data, with a timetable for outstanding material.
- **A4:** Which parts of the project can still change following consultation, and which have effectively been fixed? Explain why.
- **A5:** How will residents’ comments be recorded, considered and answered? Will you publish what changed and explain why other suggestions were not taken forward?
- **A6:** Will you hold a further meaningful consultation after principal studies and revised plans are published, but before the DCO application is submitted and while changes remain possible? Give dates and duration.
- **A7:** How will reasonable alternatives raised during consultation be investigated and assessed before the final scheme is settled? Will the evidence and reasons for rejecting alternatives be published?

## B. Need, scale, location and reasonable alternatives

- **B1:** What evidence supports the need for a data centre of the proposed size, and how has the proposed capacity been derived?
- **B2:** Why must the data centre be in this rural location rather than on brownfield land or an existing industrial, commercial or technology site? Which alternative locations were considered, and why was each rejected?
- **B3:** Could the data centre be developed separately from the solar sites and supplied through the grid, power-purchase agreements or other contracted generation? Compare costs, energy implications and local effects.
- **B4:** Could the data centre be built in stages to match confirmed customers and demonstrated demand? What evidence supports the proposed timetable and scale?
- **B5:** What local benefits genuinely depend on locating the data centre here? Distinguish firm, enforceable commitments from aspirations or estimates.
- **B6:** Have you compared ground-mounted solar with rooftop and car-park solar, suitable agrivoltaics, floating solar where appropriate, local batteries and other practical options? Show likely contribution and land saved.

## C. Local generation, batteries and a Virtual Power Plant

- **C1:** Have you assessed funding solar panels and batteries on suitable homes, farms, businesses, schools, churches, village halls and other local buildings, including solar canopies over car parks? If not, why not?
- **C2:** Could these installations be linked through a Virtual Power Plant (VPP), coordinating generation, batteries, local consumption and exports with the wider project? Explain how.
- **C3:** What solar and battery capacity could realistically be installed under low, medium and high participation scenarios? Show output by season and time of day.
- **C4:** How much could each scenario reduce ground-mounted solar land take? Give hectares and percentages, avoiding double-counting generation or stored energy.
- **C5:** What would the programme cost; who would fund, own, operate and maintain equipment; and who would pay for battery replacement?
- **C6:** What network upgrades would be needed, and could local homes and community buildings connect safely and affordably?
- **C7:** What would participating residents and organisations receive—for example funded equipment, lower electricity costs, export payments or access to surplus power—and how would benefits be guaranteed?
- **C8:** If you consider this approach impractical or unable to reduce land take, provide calculations and reasons.

## D. Agricultural land, soils, food production and farming

- **D1:** For each field and land parcel, what is the agricultural land classification: Grade 1, 2, 3a, 3b or lower? Provide detailed site-specific surveys, not just predictive maps.
- **D2:** How many hectares of Best and Most Versatile (BMV) land—Grades 1, 2 and 3a—would be affected by panels, buildings, batteries, substations, roads, cable works and other infrastructure? Separate temporary disturbance from permanent use.
- **D3:** What food and other agricultural output would be lost or reduced during construction and operation? Identify crops, production baselines and calculations.
- **D4:** How would the project affect farm businesses, field access, drainage, crop rotations, livestock, machinery, employment, tenancies and local agricultural suppliers?
- **D5:** Where is farming genuinely expected to continue between or beneath panels? What food or other agricultural products will be produced, in what quantities, and how will this be independently checked? Grass cutting or biodiversity planting alone is not food production.
- **D6:** How will topsoil, subsoil, field drains and watercourses be protected during construction, cable installation and later maintenance, and how will damage be identified and repaired?
- **D7:** How much land could realistically return to food production after decommissioning, to what standard and over what timescale? Who will verify it independently?

## E. Water resources, irrigation, drainage and flood risk

- **E1:** How much water will the data centre and associated development require each day and each year, including peak demand in hot weather and drought? Identify sources and distinguish water abstracted, used, consumed, evaporated and returned.
- **E2:** How will water supplies and wastewater systems cope during drought without disadvantaging households, farms, businesses or the environment?
- **E3:** What non-potable, recycled, treated wastewater, rainwater or other alternative supplies have been assessed, and why was the preferred source selected? If seawater is considered, provide pipeline, pumping, marine effects and cost details.
- **E4:** How will the development affect water available for agricultural irrigation, including dry periods when farmers already operate within tightly controlled abstraction and water-level arrangements?
- **E5:** Map relevant rivers, drains, ditches, dykes, culverts, pumping stations, Internal Drainage Board assets, field drains and watercourses. Explain how each will be protected, maintained and accessed.
- **E6:** Map all project areas, access routes and temporary works in tidal, river, surface-water and groundwater flood-risk areas. Include climate-change allowances and identify information gaps.
- **E7:** Will any land, road or building platform be raised? Show existing and proposed levels, height, area, material source and effects on neighbouring land and properties.
- **E8:** Show how buildings, panels, batteries, roads, fencing, bunds and raised ground could affect flood storage, water flow, runoff, groundwater and neighbouring properties.
- **E9:** Publish drainage, flood-risk and water-environment assessments, including effects on surface water, groundwater and drinking-water safeguard zones.

## F. Roads, traffic and construction

- **F1:** Show construction routes, access points and cable routes, and estimate vehicle numbers by type, road and construction stage.
- **F2:** Can local roads, bridges, verges and junctions safely accommodate construction traffic and abnormal loads? Provide condition and capacity surveys and identify required upgrades.
- **F3:** Will road and boundary conditions be recorded before work starts and surveyed afterwards? Who pays for project-related damage?
- **F4:** Will residents be reimbursed for reasonable additional travel costs caused by prolonged or repeated project road closures and diversions? Explain eligibility and claims.
- **F5:** How will emergency access, buses, school transport, farm vehicles, walkers, cyclists and access to homes and businesses be maintained?
- **F6:** Publish a coordinated timetable for roadworks and diversions, taking account of other planned developments and highway works.

## G. Landscape, appearance, design and visual effects

- **G1:** Provide realistic, matched images showing the same views before and after the whole development, from nearby homes, roads, villages, public rights of way and other public viewpoints.
- **G2:** Publish a sequence of images showing how the development would appear as people travel along principal roads and routes through affected areas, not just isolated viewpoints.
- **G3:** Show all visible elements together: data-centre buildings and rooftop plant, panels, batteries, substations, overhead lines and pylons, fences, access roads, CCTV, lighting, bunds and landscaping.
- **G4:** Provide plans, elevations and cross-sections showing existing ground levels and proposed finished levels, including raised platforms, bunds and imported soil.
- **G5:** Show a standard UK double-decker bus to scale beside buildings, rooftop plant, fences, lighting columns, battery containers, substations and overhead-line structures. Give actual dimensions.
- **G6:** The Scoping Report refers to data-centre buildings up to 25 metres high including plant. Confirm maximum height measured from existing ground level, including raised ground and rooftop equipment.
- **G7:** The current proposals describe solar panels up to 4.5 metres high. Confirm whether this remains the maximum, including tracking equipment and raised ground.
- **G8:** Show design, height, spacing and appearance of each type of overhead-line structure or pylon, with views along routes. Confirm actual proposed heights and explain the 50-metre figure raised in Council comments.
- **G9:** Show the design and dimensions of fences, CCTV poles, gates, lighting, substations and other structures.
- **G10:** Provide daytime, night-time and construction-stage images, including cranes, compounds, soil heaps and lighting. State camera positions, viewing distances and image scale.
- **G11:** Justify geographical limits used for landscape and visual assessment, including any 3km study boundary, where effects may be visible or experienced further away.

## H. Electricity demand, generation, batteries and grid infrastructure

- **H1:** Please confirm or correct the reported figures of approximately 6,000 GWh annual data-centre demand and approximately 20% of that demand being supplied by Longmarsh solar.
- **H2:** State expected average and maximum electricity demand at each development stage, separating computing demand from cooling and other building uses.
- **H3:** How much electricity will the solar sites generate each year and each month? Provide anticipated weekly figures over a year, not just the 1.3GW installed-capacity figure.
- **H4:** Provide an hourly electricity balance for a representative year, showing data-centre demand, solar generation, offshore wind or other contracted supply, batteries, grid imports and exports, backup generators and losses.
- **H5:** How much electricity will be imported from the grid each year and at peak times, including periods when solar and wind output are low?
- **H6:** Which offshore wind farms or other generators are relied upon? Is there a physical connection, power-purchase agreement or contractual matching of grid electricity? Explain what these arrangements do and do not guarantee.
- **H7:** What are the batteries’ power capacity (MW), usable storage (MWh) and discharge duration? How long could they support the data centre during low renewable output?
- **H8:** What backup generators are proposed, what fuel will they use, and how many hours are they expected to operate for testing and emergencies? Could they be used routinely?
- **H9:** Show all overhead and underground connections between the Eastern Site, Western Site, data centre and Walpole. Give route lengths, voltage, structure heights, cable widths and construction methods.
- **H10:** Explain grid connection, reinforcement, costs, timetable and who pays. How will existing electricity users and other projects be protected from adverse effects or competition for capacity?
- **H11:** Provide a whole-life carbon assessment based on expected real electricity supply, including grid imports, batteries, construction and backup generation.
- **H12:** Have Rolls-Royce Small Modular Reactors (SMRs), or other firm low-carbon power options, been assessed as an alternative or part of supply? If not, why not?
- **H13:** For an SMR option, assess potential number of units, land and cooling requirements, flood and safety constraints, delivery date, costs, grid arrangements and supply during maintenance.
- **H14:** Compare UK manufacturing, jobs, skills and export benefits of an SMR option with imported equipment and UK work involved in solar and batteries. Separate confirmed UK contracts from targets and overseas supply.

## I. Data-centre purpose, employment and local benefits

- **I1:** What work will the data centre undertake—for example AI training, AI use, cloud services or other computing? Distinguish confirmed plans from possible future uses.
- **I2:** Have customers committed to using the site or reserved capacity? If names are commercially confidential, give sectors and the proportion of capacity committed.
- **I3:** How many permanent local jobs, apprenticeships and training places are promised? How many construction jobs will be temporary, and how many are realistically expected to go to local people?
- **I4:** Will the project fund improved fibre broadband for nearby villages, homes, farms and public buildings? Map existing gaps and state exactly what will be delivered.
- **I5:** Explain the basis for claimed investment, business rates and additional jobs, identifying which benefits are guaranteed and enforceable and which are estimates.

## J. Cooling, waste heat and energy efficiency

- **J1:** Explain exactly how the data centre will be cooled, including closed-loop circuits, chillers, dry coolers, cooling towers and evaporative equipment.
- **J2:** Have you assessed recovering data-centre waste heat and supplying it through insulated pipes to nearby homes, schools, churches, care homes, swimming pools, farms or businesses?
- **J3:** How much useful heat could be recovered, at what temperature, and what heat pumps, pipes and storage would be needed? Map potential users and distances.
- **J4:** Could heat recovery be incorporated into the original design? How would unused heat be rejected in summer or when a heat network is unavailable?
- **J5:** Would the developer fund the heat network and provide heat at a fair, transparent price? If heat recovery or district heating is rejected, provide evidence and reasons.

## K. Noise, lighting, glare, air quality and health

- **K1:** Publish baseline noise readings and predict noise from cooling equipment, transformers, batteries, substations, traffic and generators, including at night and at nearby homes.
- **K2:** Set clear noise limits, monitoring arrangements, public reporting and corrective action if limits are exceeded.
- **K3:** Show security and operational lighting at night from nearby homes, roads and public places. Explain how light spill will be prevented.
- **K4:** Assess dust, traffic pollution and generator emissions, including expected frequency and duration of generator testing.
- **K5:** Assess solar-panel reflections at roads, junctions, homes, footpaths, farms and workplaces, including low winter sun and drivers’ lines of sight.
- **K6:** Explain what changes will be made if noise, glare or light pollution is unacceptable, and how complaints and corrective action will be handled.
- **K7:** Assess cumulative effects on residents’ health and wellbeing from noise, dust, traffic, lighting, loss of access to countryside and other relevant effects.
- **K8:** Could an independent sound-and-light demonstration be arranged before the application is submitted? If not, provide the closest reliable alternative.

## L. Wildlife, habitats and ecological networks

- **L1:** Publish wildlife and habitat surveys for both sites and all cable, road and overhead-line routes, including appropriate seasonal surveys.
- **L2:** Map the extent and condition of habitats, including wetlands, ditches, watercourses, hedgerows, grasslands, woodland, mature trees and farmland habitats.
- **L3:** Identify protected sites and species, priority habitats, ancient woodland, veteran trees, irreplaceable habitats and locally important habitat corridors.
- **L4:** Which habitats will be directly lost, damaged, fragmented or disturbed during construction and operation? Assess effects of panels, fencing, lighting, noise, roads and other infrastructure.
- **L5:** Identify the heronry raised by local residents, show its location and assess potential effects on nesting, feeding and flight paths.
- **L6:** How will connections between habitats be protected and wildlife movement maintained across and around the development?
- **L7:** What alternatives to damaging important habitats have been considered, and how has avoidance been prioritised before mitigation or compensation?
- **L8:** What habitat losses cannot realistically be recreated, and how will those losses be treated in the assessment?
- **L9:** Show what will be protected, restored or improved for wildlife, with measurable targets, long-term funding and monitoring.
- **L10:** Who will monitor habitat condition and wildlife outcomes, for how long, and what enforceable action will follow if commitments are not met?
- **L11:** How will the development affect the Norfolk Local Nature Recovery Strategy and wider ecological networks, including connections across county boundaries?

## M. Fire, emergency services, security and aviation

- **M1:** Explain battery fire safety, including detection, suppression, separation distances, emergency access and water supply.
- **M2:** Assess credible fire, smoke, explosion and toxic-release incidents and possible effects on nearby homes, farms, roads, watercourses and habitats.
- **M3:** Assess wildfire risk from panels, vegetation, batteries, transformers, fuel and backup generators, including fire breaks and potential spread beyond the site.
- **M4:** What additional staff, equipment, training and facilities would local fire, ambulance, police and health services need? Identify costs and who will fund them.
- **M5:** Will the developer meet additional costs of services required because of the project and secure funding for the life of the development? How will residents be protected from meeting those additional costs through local Council Tax?
- **M6:** Explain how emergency plans, public warnings, evacuation advice and incident response will be tested and kept up to date.
- **M7:** Have the Ministry of Defence, RAF Marham, the Civil Aviation Authority and other aviation bodies been consulted? What issues have they raised about glare, radar, communications, structure heights and construction cranes?
- **M8:** Explain physical and cyber-security arrangements and how serious incidents would be coordinated with emergency services and local authorities.

## N. Regional cumulative effects, property values and local accountability

- **N1:** Assess Longmarsh alongside other existing, approved, proposed, paused and early-stage energy and infrastructure projects across Norfolk, Lincolnshire and Cambridgeshire, including the wider Fenland agricultural area. Identify included projects and explain exclusions.
- **N2:** Provide a regional map and schedule showing each project’s status, total area, actual panel area, battery and substation land, cable routes and overhead lines. Distinguish active, paused, approved and early-stage proposals.
- **N3:** For each relevant project, show the area of Grade 1, 2 and 3a land involved, distinguishing detailed survey results from predictive estimates. Provide regional totals of BMV land affected.
- **N4:** Assess combined effects across the region on food production, farm businesses, agricultural viability, soil, irrigation and water availability, drainage, flooding, roads, wildlife habitats, landscape, homes, local services and communities.
- **N5:** Explain how projects’ grid connections, substations, pylons and cable routes will fit together, whether they compete for capacity and whether they create overlapping construction disruption.
- **N6:** How will the cumulative assessment recognise that Fenland agricultural, drainage and ecological systems cross county boundaries and cannot properly be assessed by looking at Norfolk alone?
- **N7:** What independent assessment has been made of likely effects on the value and saleability of nearby homes? What geographical area and property types were included, and how were effects of the data centre, solar arrays, pylons, substations, traffic, noise, lighting and cumulative impacts assessed?
- **N8:** Will you fund an independent baseline study of local property values before any decision or works, using actual comparable sales and independent valuations, and publish its method and results? Will it be updated at consent, construction and operational stages?
- **N9:** If a marked loss in property value is demonstrably attributable to the development, what compensation, purchase, property-protection or other redress will be available? If no scheme is proposed, explain why and identify who made that decision.
- **N10:** What information will be provided about Council Tax band reviews where a material physical change in the locality has a demonstrable effect on property value? Will you support a clear route for residents to obtain VOA guidance and understand the evidence required?
- **N11:** What can residents, farms and businesses claim if the project causes loss or damage? Explain eligibility, evidence, payment times and independent dispute resolution.
- **N12:** Will there be a named local contact, public complaints process and route for independent escalation of unresolved issues?
- **N13:** What financial guarantees will secure mitigation, monitoring, compensation, road repairs and long-term obligations, including if the developer or operator fails or changes ownership?
- **N14:** Identify who will coordinate the regional assessment, its geographical scope, publication timetable and how findings will influence decisions on individual projects.

## O. Construction safeguards, permissions and no premature works

- **O1:** List all physical works proposed before and during construction, distinguishing non-intrusive surveys from site clearance, vegetation removal, fencing, access roads, groundworks, drainage, land raising, cable works and grid infrastructure.
- **O2:** Will you commit that no enabling or construction works will begin until reasonable alternatives have been fully explored and reported, and meaningful consultation on the final scheme has taken place while changes remain possible?
- **O3:** Will you also confirm that no such works will begin until all necessary statutory consents and permissions have been granted and applicable pre-commencement requirements satisfied?
- **O4:** Will the commitment cover site clearance, vegetation removal, fencing, access roads, groundworks, drainage and land-level changes, cable routes, substations and associated grid works? Identify any activities you believe could lawfully begin earlier and the authority and safeguards relied upon.
- **O5:** Which authority will secure and enforce this commitment? Will it be legally secured rather than left as a voluntary promise?
- **O6:** If you will not give the requested commitment, explain precisely what work could start earlier, under what authority, and what opportunity residents would have to challenge or comment before it takes place.

## P. Decommissioning, restoration and long-term responsibility

- **P1:** At the end of the project, what will be removed and what will remain, including the data centre, substations, overhead lines, cables, roads and batteries?
- **P2:** How much land could return to farming, to what standard and over what timescale? Who will inspect and verify restoration independently?
- **P3:** Provide the full decommissioning and restoration cost and explain what bond, guarantee or other financial security will be in place if the company fails.
- **P4:** Who will maintain and pay for infrastructure left in place after other parts of the project are decommissioned?

### Source and status note

These questions seek evidence and clarification; they do not assume that every possible impact will occur. The applicant’s current proposals page describes solar panels up to 4.5 metres high, battery containers up to 4.5 metres, agricultural fencing up to 2.5 metres, CCTV poles up to 5 metres and palisade fencing up to 3 metres around high-voltage areas. The October 2026 Scoping Report describes data-centre buildings up to 25 metres including plant. The applicant should confirm these dimensions in the final design and show combined heights from existing ground level, including any proposed ground raising.

The earlier suggestion of 15-metre solar panels and a four-double-decker-bus building height is not treated as fact. The questions ask the applicant to confirm actual dimensions and illustrate them to scale. References to other projects and regional effects are requests for assessment, not assertions that every project will proceed or that every identified impact will occur.
