Response to the Longmarsh Green Energy consultation (EN0110048)

Name: [your name]. Address or postcode: [your address]. Responding as: [your capacity].

I object to the Longmarsh proposal as presented. It would take about 4,700 acres of farmland, much of it reported to be grade one, the highest quality. National policy asks developers to minimise impacts on best and most versatile land and to prefer poorer-quality land and previously developed sites. [Check before quoting: EN-1 (2025) paragraph 5.11.12 and EN-3 (2025) paragraph 2.10.21.] I have seen no evidence that brownfield alternatives were assessed and ruled out.

The scheme bundles up to 1.3 GW of solar and storage with a 1 GW AI data centre campus. The data centre is a different kind of development, with its own demands on power, water, noise and land. Its occupier has not been named, and no visuals have been published. Communities cannot judge impacts they cannot see.

This is not temporary. The developer says the solar would run for about 60 years, and that the data centre, substation, batteries and western cable route would then stay.

Food security, the compulsory purchase risk to farms, and the pylons crossing working farmland are not outweighed by the benefits shown so far.

I ask the developer to: (1) publish the brownfield and lower-grade land site selection evidence; (2) name the data centre occupier and publish visuals; (3) show what share of the solar output serves the data centre; (4) publish water use, cooling and noise assessments; (5) consider separating the data centre from the solar scheme and phasing both on lower-grade land.

Please record this as an objection and reply to each point in the consultation report.

[your name]
