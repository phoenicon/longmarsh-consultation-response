## Who is responding

I object to the Longmarsh Green Energy proposal in its current form. Please record this response and answer it in the consultation report. [Add your own connection to the area, as true for you.]

I am not opposed to solar, storage or data centres in principle. I am opposed to placing a very large combined scheme on productive farmland without evidence that better sites were considered first. The developer is referred to as both EPL Norfolk Limited and Longmarsh Green Energy, and I treat both as the applicant.

## 1. Grade one land and food security

The scheme covers about 4,700 acres (about 1,900 hectares) of farmland across two sites. Natural England is reported to rate much of this land as grade one, its highest category. The full Agricultural Land Classification breakdown has not been published, so the share of the site that is best and most versatile land (grades 1, 2 and 3a) is not yet known.

National policy asks applicants to minimise impacts on best and most versatile land and to prefer poorer-quality land. [Check before quoting: EN-1 (2025) paragraph 5.11.12, *"Applicants should seek to minimise impacts on the best and most versatile agricultural land"*; EN-3 (2025) paragraph 2.10.21, *"poorer quality land should be preferred to higher quality land"*. EN-3 (2025) paragraph 2.10.22 is said to say solar on such land is not prohibited outright. Do not add NPPF paragraph numbers until you have checked the current NPPF.]

The consultation material does not include:
- a parcel-by-parcel Agricultural Land Classification survey;
- the site selection process, showing which brownfield and lower-grade sites were assessed and why each was rejected;
- an estimate of the food production lost each year.

I ask the developer to publish all three before it applies for a Development Consent Order.

## 2. Scale and bundling

The proposal combines up to 1.3 GW of solar and storage with a 1 GW AI data centre campus across two sites. They would be linked by overhead lines and underground cable to Walpole substation, with grid connection expected from 2031. Presenting this as one "green energy" project hides two very different developments.

**It is not temporary.** The developer's proposals say the solar would run for about 60 years. After that, the Western Site data centre, substation, battery storage and Western Cable Route are intended to stay. [Check before quoting: from the developer's proposals page as reported in one draft.]

**The data centre is unexplained.** The occupier has not been named and no visuals have been published. It is unclear what share of the solar output would serve the data centre rather than the grid. Communities cannot judge impacts they cannot see.

**The physical scale needs showing.** [Check before quoting: as quoted in the Stop Longmarsh campaign's Schedule A, the developer's proposals page describes solar panels up to 4.5 metres high and battery containers up to 4.5 metres, and its October 2026 Scoping Report describes data-centre buildings up to 25 metres including plant.] I ask the developer to confirm these maximum heights, measured from existing ground level and including any raised ground or rooftop equipment, and to publish matched before-and-after images from the same viewpoints.

**Installed solar capacity is not the same as supply.** Solar runs only part of the time; a data centre runs continuously. I ask the developer to publish an hour-by-hour energy model showing generation, consumption, storage, grid imports and exports. [Check before quoting: one draft's illustrative calculation, using an assumed 11% solar capacity factor and 90% data centre load, puts on-site solar at about 16% of annual demand. These are assumptions, not the developer's figures.]

**300 metres.** The developer has said the data centre would be at least 300 metres from any resident. I ask for the evidence that this is enough for noise, light and visual impact.

## 3. Alternatives

I ask that the consultation report shows each option considered, the criteria used and the reason it was rejected:
- brownfield and industrial sites for the data centre;
- lower-grade land (3b, 4, 5) for the solar, ranked by distance to Walpole;
- separating the solar and the data centre;
- a smaller first phase tied to evidenced demand;
- rooftop, car park and industrial-site solar.

[Check before quoting: the Infrastructure Planning (Environmental Impact Assessment) Regulations 2017, regulation 14 and schedule 4, are said to require the applicant to describe the reasonable alternatives it studied and its main reasons for the option chosen.]

## 4. Compulsory purchase, pylons and farm succession

The developer proposes overhead lines and underground cable between the two sites and Walpole: a Western route of about 8 km, mostly overhead, and an Eastern route of about 8 km underground and 8 km overhead. [Check before quoting: approximate lengths from the developer's proposals as reported in one draft.]

Objectors have reported compulsory purchase worries and pylons crossing working farms. [Check before quoting: under section 122(3) of the Planning Act 2008, compulsory acquisition needs *"a compelling case in the public interest"*.]

I ask the developer to state:
- whether compulsory acquisition is being sought, over which land and rights;
- how many farm businesses would lose all or part of their land;
- what compensation, independent advice and relocation support is offered;
- whether undergrounding the whole route was considered, and at what cost.

## 5. Water, power, noise and cumulative impact

The developer says it will use closed-loop liquid cooling to limit water use. [Check before quoting: its consultation leaflet is reported to say a "sealed closed-loop cooling system" means "no significant impact on local water supply".] Closed-loop describes how coolant circulates. It does not by itself say how much water the site uses, how heat is finally rejected, or how much extra water is needed in a heatwave. [Check before quoting: in July 2026, Water UK, the water industry's trade body, reportedly warned that there is not enough water for the UK's data centre plans (The Guardian, 21 July 2026).]

Before the application, I ask for:
- the cooling design, heat rejection method, and annual and peak water use, with the source, including during summer heatwaves;
- a flood risk and surface-water drainage assessment covering the Fenland drainage network [check before quoting: parts of the site are reported to lie in Flood Zone 3];
- the power demand profile;
- noise predictions at the nearest homes by day and night, including low-frequency noise;
- details of the backup generators, their testing, emissions and fuel storage;
- the effect on nearby protected sites and heritage, and why the proposed buffers are enough [check before quoting: reported to include Islington Heronry SSSI, with a 100 m buffer, Birch Wood ancient woodland and the Fincham Conservation Area];
- construction traffic and haul routes through Tilney St Lawrence, Tilney cum Islington, Fincham and Barton Bendish;
- the cumulative effect with other energy and infrastructure projects, assessed at two scales:
  - **in and around West Norfolk:** the borough council's 16 November 2026 presentation lists The Droves Solar Farm, Anglian Water's Fens Reservoir, Redmere Wind Farm, Medworth Energy from Waste and National Grid's Eastern Green Link 3 and 4 and Grimsby to Walpole projects;
  - **across Norfolk as a region:** major solar proposals including High Grove, The Droves and East Pye, and the paused Tasway proposal [check before quoting: as listed in the Stop Longmarsh campaign's letter to the developer, which notes that published project areas are measured on different bases and should not simply be added together].

The site stretches about 29 km and wraps around a string of villages, so the combined effect needs assessing as a whole.

1. Record this as an objection and answer each point in the consultation report.
2. Publish a parcel-by-parcel Agricultural Land Classification survey.
3. Publish the site selection evidence, including brownfield and lower-grade alternatives.
4. Name the data centre occupier and publish visuals and a landscape assessment.
5. Publish an hour-by-hour energy model.
6. Publish water, cooling, noise and construction traffic assessments.
7. Say whether compulsory acquisition is intended, and what protection affected owners and tenants will have.
8. Show how environmental commitments will be legally secured and independently monitored.
