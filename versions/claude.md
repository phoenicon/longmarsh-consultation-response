## 1. Key facts about the scheme

- **Applicant.** Referred to as both EPL Norfolk Limited and Longmarsh Green Energy (LGE). Sources: [Lynn News, 23 Nov 2026](https://www.lynnnews.co.uk/news/consultation-on-huge-solar-farm-and-data-centre-closes-this-9492448/), [Fenland Citizen](https://www.fenlandcitizen.co.uk/news/villagers-fear-ai-data-centre-and-solar-farm-plans-will-des-9488205/).
- **Scope.** Up to 1.3GW of solar generation and battery storage plus a 1GW data centre campus, across two sites: one northeast of Tilney St Lawrence, one near Fincham and Barton Bendish. The data centre would power AI. Source: [Lynn News](https://www.lynnnews.co.uk/news/consultation-on-huge-solar-farm-and-data-centre-closes-this-9492448/).
- **Size.** About 4,695 acres (1,900 hectares) of farmland across two sites. Source: [BBC report via now.solar](https://now.solar/2026/11/23/locals-and-council-to-fight-norfolk-data-centre-and-solar-farm-bbc/). The Farmers Guardian headline figure is £25 billion for the whole development.

Source: [Digital Urban](https://digitalurban.org/blog/2026/10/29/longmarsh-mapping-a-solar-farm-and-data-centre-for-the-villages-it-surrounds/).
- **Grid connection.** To Walpole substation, expected from 2031. Source: [Lynn News](https://www.lynnnews.co.uk/news/consultation-on-huge-solar-farm-and-data-centre-closes-this-9492448/).
- **Data centre separation.** Longmarsh told the Lynn News the data centre campus would be at least 300 metres from any resident. Source: [Lynn News, village hall report](https://www.lynnnews.co.uk/news/concerned-residents-pack-into-village-hall-over-solar-and-da-9489740/).
- **Occupier and visuals.** The data centre occupier has not been named and no visuals have been published; residents say they are "angry" about this. Source: [Lynn News, 23 Nov](https://www.lynnnews.co.uk/news/consultation-on-huge-solar-farm-and-data-centre-closes-this-9492448/).
- **Decision route.** Nationally significant infrastructure project, so ministers decide, not the council. Consultation ends 26 November 2026; Development Consent Order application due by April 2027; examination about six months; decision possible by July 2028. Sources: [now.solar/BBC](https://now.solar/2026/11/23/locals-and-council-to-fight-norfolk-data-centre-and-solar-farm-bbc/), [Resultsense](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-11-04-longmarsh-norfolk-meeting-data-centre/).

## 2. Arguments against, by theme

### Land and food security
- Natural England rates the land grade one, its highest category ([Resultsense](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-11-04-longmarsh-norfolk-meeting-data-centre/), [BBC via now.solar](https://now.solar/2026/11/23/locals-and-council-to-fight-norfolk-data-centre-and-solar-farm-bbc/)).
- A neighbouring farmer, James Kitchen, says his silt soil kept yields up through this year's drought ([Resultsense](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-11-04-longmarsh-norfolk-meeting-data-centre/)).
- No agricultural land classification by parcel, site selection evidence or food-production-lost estimate has been seen in the consultation material I found.
- Policy substance (UNVERIFIED, general knowledge): national planning policy defines best and most versatile land as grades 1, 2 and 3a and says to avoid it where possible, preferring brownfield and non-agricultural land. Exact wording and paragraph numbers in the NPPF, EN-1 and EN-3 need checking.

### Scale and the data centre
- Two different developments are bundled under one "green energy" label. The data centre has its own land take, cooling, noise, water, traffic and construction impacts.
- Occupier unnamed, no visuals, and no stated split of solar output between the data centre and the grid.
- 300 metres separation: the evidence it is enough for noise, light and visual impact has not been published.
- One councillor called the solar a "Trojan horse" for the data centre ([BBC via now.solar](https://now.solar/2026/11/23/locals-and-council-to-fight-norfolk-data-centre-and-solar-farm-bbc/)).

### Alternatives
- Objectors accept data centres and solar in principle but want them smaller and on brownfield land ([Resultsense](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-11-04-longmarsh-norfolk-meeting-data-centre/)).
- Options the developer should show it tested: brownfield sites for the data centre, lower-grade land (3b, 4, 5) for solar, separating the two developments, phasing, and rooftop and industrial-site solar.

### Compulsory purchase, pylons and succession
- Objectors report compulsory purchase worries and three pylons crossing one neighbouring farm ([BBC via now.solar](https://now.solar/2026/11/23/locals-and-council-to-fight-norfolk-data-centre-and-solar-farm-bbc/)).
- General reasoning (UNVERIFIED, not from a source): easements and pylons fragment fields, restrict cultivation and reduce land value, and partial loss can make a farm unviable.

### Water, power, noise, cumulative impact
- General concern that large data centres can use significant power and water; the Lynn News notes noise and energy and water use as concerns ([Lynn News](https://www.lynnnews.co.uk/news/consultation-on-huge-solar-farm-and-data-centre-closes-this-9492448/)). ## 5. Final draft: full response

**Who is responding.** I object to the Longmarsh Green Energy proposal in its current form, and I ask that this response is recorded and answered in the consultation report. [Add your own capacity and experience, exactly as true for you.] I am not opposed to solar, storage or data centres in principle. I am opposed to placing a very large combined scheme on productive, high-grade farmland without evidence that better sites were considered first. The developer is referred to as both EPL Norfolk Limited and Longmarsh Green Energy; I treat both as the applicant.

**Grade one land and food security.** The scheme covers about 4,695 acres (1,900 hectares) of farmland, and Natural England classes much of the land as grade one, its highest category. Grades 1, 2 and 3a are defined in national planning policy as best and most versatile land. National policy asks developers to avoid it where possible and to prefer brownfield and non-agricultural sites. [Check the exact wording and paragraph numbers in EN-1, EN-3 and the NPPF.] The consultation material I have seen does not show an agricultural land classification survey for each parcel, the site selection process including which brownfield and lower-grade sites were assessed and why each was rejected, or an estimate of the food production lost per year.

**Scale and bundling.** The proposal combines up to 1.3GW of solar and storage with a 1GW data centre campus across two sites, linked by overhead lines and underground cable to the Walpole substation, with a grid connection expected from 2031. Presenting this as one "green energy" project hides two different developments. The occupier is not named, no visuals have been published, and it is unclear what share of the solar output would serve the data centre. The developer has said the data centre would sit at least 300 metres from any resident; I ask for the evidence that this is enough for noise, light and visual impact.

**Alternatives.** I ask that the consultation report shows each option considered, the criteria and the reason for rejection: brownfield sites for the data centre; lower-grade land for the solar, ranked by distance to Walpole; separating the two developments; a smaller first phase; and rooftop, car park and industrial-site solar.

**Compulsory purchase, pylons and farm succession.** I ask the developer to state which landowners and tenants are affected; whether compulsory purchase is being sought and over how much land; how many farm businesses lose all or part of their land and what compensation and relocation support is offered; and whether undergrounding the full route was considered.

**Water, power, noise and cumulative impact.** Before the application, I ask for the cooling method and annual water use with its source; the power demand profile; a noise assessment including low-frequency noise at the nearest homes; construction traffic and haul routes through Tilney St Lawrence, Tilney cum Islington, Fincham and Barton Bendish; and the cumulative effect with other energy projects. The site runs about 29 km and wraps around a string of villages, so the combined effect needs assessing as a whole.

## 6. Questions for the developer

1. What share of the 1,900 hectares is grade one, two and 3a land, by parcel?
2. Which brownfield and lower-grade sites were assessed, against what criteria, and why was each rejected?
3. Who will occupy the data centre, and when will that be made public?
4. When will visuals, photomontages and a landscape assessment be published?
5. What share of solar output will serve the data centre, and what share will go to the grid?
6. What are the expected annual water use, the cooling method and the water source?
7. What are the noise predictions at the nearest homes, and why is 300 metres the chosen separation?
8. Is compulsory purchase being sought, over which land, and how many farm businesses lose all or part of their holding?
9. Was undergrounding of the whole grid route considered, and what would it cost?
10. Why are the solar and the data centre one application, and what would happen to the solar if the data centre did not go ahead?
11. What is the annual food production lost, and what is the decommissioning and land restoration plan, with who pays and when?
12. What community benefit is proposed, and how does it compare with the harm to affected villages and farms?

## 7. What has stopped other data centres (precedents)

- **Iver / West London Technology Park, Buckinghamshire.** The Secretary of State refused a 147MW hyperscale scheme in October 2023 over Green Belt harm, character and appearance, and availability of alternative sites ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/woodlands-data-centre-refusal-key-observations), [UK Planning Law Blog](https://www.planninglawblog.com/data-centre-refusal-highlights-challenges/)).
- **Edinburgh, South Gyle.** Councillors unanimously refused an AI data centre (about 210MW) on 4 February 2026 on climate grounds, against officer advice; now with Scottish Ministers on appeal ([Holyrood](https://www.holyrood.com/news/view,edinburgh-council-refuses-planning-permission-for-ai-data-centre), [DC Blueprint register](https://dcblueprint.com/insights/europe-data-centre-refusals-register-2026)).
- **Amlwch, Anglesey.** Refused on 20 April 2026 for not enough information on landscape, ecology and other matters ([DC Blueprint register](https://dcblueprint.com/insights/europe-data-centre-refusals-register-2026)).
- **Hertsmere (Ark, about £2.5bn).** Deferred on 5 November 2026 over water supply, design and Section 106 contributions ([Planning Geek](https://www.planninggeek.co.uk/2026/hertsmere-data-centre/)).
- **Buckinghamshire, Greystoke Land.** In January 2026 the Government admitted a "serious logical error" in granting permission after the council had refused twice, so the decision is likely to be quashed ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/planning-permission-for-buckinghamshire-data-center-likely-to-be-quashed-after-uk-government-admits-serious-logical-error/)).
- **Waterford, Ireland.** Refused in October 2026 because the applicant did not show existing grid capacity, and over village character ([Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/ireland/2026/10/20/contentious-data-centres-plan-in-co-waterford-is-refused-planning-permission/)).
- **Spain and the Netherlands.** Spain has had grid refusals and four Dutch municipalities wrote bans into zoning plans ([DC Blueprint register](https://dcblueprint.com/insights/europe-data-centre-refusals-register-2026)).
- **United States.** Data Center Watch counts at least 120 projects, about $198bn, blocked or delayed by local opposition in the first half of 2026 ([Mingtiandi](https://mingtiandi.com/real-estate/data-centres/nextdc-freezes-projects-near-homes-after-melbourne-backlash)); Fortune reports cancellations rising from six in 2024 to 25 in 2025 ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/05/18/communities-are-blocking-billions-in-data-centers-big-tech-has-wagered-1-trillion-otherwise/)).
- **Counter-signals.** The UK Government rejected a freeze on new data centre approvals and published planning rules strengthening support for data centres and AI Growth Zones ([Construction Magazine](https://www.constructionmagazine.uk/2026/08/uk-data-centre-construction-2026-government-rejects-freeze.html)). A December 2024 decision letter for Court Lane, Iver allowed an appeal and granted permission for a data centre (seen only as a search snippet).

Not verified: the date of the Three Rivers (Abbots Langley) 96MW refusal, which a search snippet gave as 18 January without a clear year.
